Understanding the scale of the camps

Many people know that the Nazi concentration and extermination camps existed, but few have a sense of their physical size. This map places the documented footprint of a camp, at its true size, over a place you know, such as your own town, so that its scale can be understood in familiar terms.

No map can convey the suffering of the people imprisoned and murdered in these camps. The outlines are offered as an aid to learning and remembrance, alongside the history kept by the memorials at each site.

How it works

  1. 1

    Choose a camp

    Each camp is drawn from its documented perimeter and the barracks, ruins, railways and watchtowers recorded on OpenStreetMap.

  2. 2

    Place it over a familiar location

    Search for a town or address, use your location, or move the outline by hand. It is redrawn at its true size wherever it is placed, without distortion from the map projection.

  3. 3

    Read about each layer

    Structures can be shown or hidden. The i button next to each layer explains what it was and what happened there.

Camps on the map

The outlines below are drawn at the same scale. Select a camp to view it on the map.

Outlines follow what is mapped today. Where the original perimeter does not survive, the outline is the memorial grounds, which may be smaller than the camp was. Each camp's page says which.

What the layers show

The features of each camp are grouped into the same kinds of layers. On the map, each layer also has a note about its role at that particular camp.

Purpose

A figure such as 1.69 km², or 236 football pitches, is easy to read but hard to picture. Seeing the outline of a camp across streets one knows can make its size, and the scale of the crimes committed there, easier to grasp. The project is intended for education and remembrance, for example in classrooms and in preparation for a visit to a memorial.

Scale cannot describe the human loss. This map is an educational spatial comparison and cannot represent the lives, suffering, and histories of the people persecuted and murdered by Nazi Germany. For the history of each site, please refer to the memorials and museums linked for every camp.

Data and method

Geometry © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Each shape is stored as metres from the camp's centre and re-projected around wherever you place it, the same idea thetruesize.com uses for countries. Victim figures come from the memorial or museum responsible for each site. The code is open source on GitHub.
